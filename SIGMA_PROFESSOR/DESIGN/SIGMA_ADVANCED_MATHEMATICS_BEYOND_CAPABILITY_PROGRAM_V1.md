## Capability dependency map

The IDs below are program identifiers, not claims of admission and not a required numerical execution order.

### MATH-R0 — representation, exactness, and proof obligations

- `MATH-R0-EXPR` — represent bounded mathematical expressions and operator structure natively.
- `MATH-R0-DOMAIN` — carry explicit domain/precondition records; reject operations when required conditions are absent.
- `MATH-R0-EXACT` — exact integer/rational arithmetic substrate; approximation must be explicitly marked.
- `MATH-R0-EVIDENCE` — bind derived mathematical claims to runtime input/provenance and proof/verification status.
- `MATH-R0-COUNTEREXAMPLE` — test bounded universal candidates against dynamic counterexamples without treating finite search as a general proof.

These capabilities are prerequisites for most higher mathematical work.

### MATH-R1 — arithmetic, algebra, discrete structures

- integer divisibility, gcd/extended-gcd, modular arithmetic;
- exact rational normalization;
- polynomial representation and bounded polynomial operations;
- algebraic rewrite/equivalence checks with explicit side conditions;
- finite equation/system solving in admitted classes;
- combinatorial counting in admitted finite classes;
- recurrence evaluation in bounded/admitted forms;
- graph and finite discrete-structure operations;
- finite relation/equivalence/order checks.

No general symbolic solver may be claimed from a finite set of rewrite rules.

### MATH-R2 — linear algebra and geometry substrate

- vector and matrix operations;
- exact or explicitly approximate Gaussian elimination;
- rank, null-space and solution verification in admitted numeric domains;
- linear transformations and composition;
- determinant/eigenvalue capabilities only in explicitly admitted classes;
- metric/coordinate transformations with domain checks;
- finite or algebraically specified geometric predicates.

### MATH-R3 — calculus and real/complex analysis

Capabilities must be decomposed rather than called a universal calculus engine:

- sequence/function evaluation under explicit domains;
- symbolic derivative rules with preconditions and independent verification cases;
- antiderivative/transformation rules in admitted function classes;
- numerical integration with explicit error/step controls when approximation is used;
- limit/continuity proof-obligation handling for admitted templates;
- convergence tests as separate capabilities with exact hypotheses;
- complex arithmetic and admitted analytic transformations;
- theorem use only when the theorem's hypotheses are represented and checked.

A correct derivative/integral on one expression is not proof of general calculus capability.

### MATH-R4 — differential equations, dynamics, probability, optimization

- verify candidate ODE/PDE solutions against equations and boundary/initial conditions;
- solve only explicitly admitted equation families;
- represent discrete dynamical evolution and stability tests in admitted cases;
- exact finite probability and combinatorial probability;
- distribution/moment operations in admitted families;
- stochastic-process reasoning only after probability-state representation is admitted;
- optimization in finite, linear, convex, or other separately admitted classes;
- numerical optimization must expose termination/error conditions.

### MATH-R5 — abstract algebra, topology, measure, functional analysis

- finite-group/ring/field property checks before broader symbolic structural proofs;
- homomorphism/composition/invariant checks under explicit structures;
- topology/metric-space predicates in finite or formally represented cases;
- sigma-algebra/measure operations only after set/proof dependencies are admitted;
- functional-analysis capabilities decomposed into represented spaces, operators, norms and separately admitted theorem schemas;
- no theorem family is admitted merely by storing its name or conclusion.

### MATH-R6 — advanced geometry and number theory

- elementary and computational number-theory algorithms;
- prime/divisibility/congruence capabilities in admitted exact domains;
- Diophantine verification/search capabilities with boundedness explicitly stated;
- differential-geometry computations in admitted coordinate representations;
- algebraic-geometry manipulations only after polynomial/algebraic structure dependencies;
- analytic/algebraic number-theory theorem use must verify hypotheses and preserve provenance;
- conjectures remain conjectures unless separately proven in the active formal/evidence framework.

### MATH-R7 — category, homological/compositional and higher abstractions

- typed objects/morphisms and compositional validity;
- commutative-diagram checks in finite represented diagrams;
- functor/natural-transformation checks in admitted finite/formal schemas;
- chain-complex and homology computations in finite admitted complexes;
- higher abstraction must reuse lower admitted representation/proof capabilities rather than bypass them with labels.

### MATH-R8 — proof, theorem discovery, and research behavior

This is the "beyond" frontier and has the strongest dependencies.

- proof-step verification inside explicitly represented proof systems;
- proof search only in bounded/admitted calculi;
- theorem-hypothesis matching with explicit scope limits;
- competing-proof/counterexample handling;
- conjecture candidate generation from native evidence only after concept/evidence dependencies are admitted;
- research-gap formation only after native gap detection/curiosity dependencies are admitted;
- literature/source evidence must preserve provenance and uncertainty;
- revalidation must be able to reopen or supersede mathematical knowledge when new evidence invalidates assumptions.

`GENERAL_AUTONOMOUS_MATHEMATICAL_RESEARCH=NOT_PROVEN` until a dedicated admission proves it.

## Mathematics source/corpus rule

A curriculum, textbook, paper, proof archive, or prior HKA record may be ingested only as evidence/raw material under provenance. It is never itself proof of a SIGMA capability.

Host may mechanically:

- copy exact source bytes;
- hash source bytes;
- persist document/segment identities;
- transport exact fetched data;
- invoke the locked runtime.

Host must not:

- choose the mathematical topic for SIGMA;
- summarize the source for SIGMA;
- solve the exercise first;
- label a result true on SIGMA's behalf;
- generate the concept or proof candidate that SIGMA is supposed to generate;
- select the curriculum priority.

## Admission requirements for every mathematics capability

Minimum:

- native `.sigma` source;
- exact source SHA256;
- compile with locked `sigmac`;
- exact bytecode SHA256;
- run with locked VM;
- at least two materially different dynamic inputs when practical;
- output/state changes because input changes;
- negative/counterexample case;
- explicit domain/precondition failure where relevant;
- persistent-state/restart tests for learned/revalidated mathematical knowledge;
- boundedness/step-limit characterization;
- host-substitution audit;
- claim-scope review;
- admission PASS/FAIL.

For approximate numerical capability also require:

- exact-versus-approximate status;
- precision/tolerance input;
- error or residual evidence appropriate to the admitted class;
- a case where tolerance/precondition is not met and SIGMA does not falsely promote the result.

For proof/theorem capability also require:

- hypothesis representation;
- a valid case;
- an invalid proof/counterexample case;
- no theorem promotion from name/lookup alone;
- exact distinction between verified step sequence, theorem schema, conjecture and external citation.
